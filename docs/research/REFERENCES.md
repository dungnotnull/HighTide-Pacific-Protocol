## 1. Pacific sea-level data and observation

**[PDH-SEA] Pacific Data Hub — Climate Change, Disasters and Risks** — The Pacific Community (SPC) — https://pacificdata.org/topic/climate-change-disasters-and-risks — *Relevance:* Regional catalogue of Pacific climate and sea-level indicator datasets. — *Supports:* Primary data source for Pacific sea-level inputs and country risk profiles.

**[NOAA-SLT] Sea Level Trends (Tides and Currents)** — NOAA Center for Operational Oceanographic Products and Services — https://tidesandcurrents.noaa.gov/sltrends/slrmap.html — *Relevance:* Official relative sea-level trend estimates from long-term tide gauges. — *Supports:* Benchmark and validation for trend-adjusted trigger thresholds.

**[PSMSL] Permanent Service for Mean Sea Level — Tide Gauge Data** — PSMSL (NOC, UK) — https://psmsl.org/data/obtaining/ — *Relevance:* Global data bank of monthly/annual mean sea level from 2,250+ tide gauge stations. — *Supports:* Long-term tide gauge records used for baseline trend estimation.

**[NASA-SL] NASA Sea Level Change Portal** — NASA — https://sealevel.nasa.gov/ — *Relevance:* Satellite altimetry data, global mean sea level, and IPCC AR6 sea-level projection tool. — *Supports:* Authoritative sea-level observations and projections for trigger calibration.

**[WB-CCKP] Climate Change Knowledge Portal** — World Bank Group — https://climateknowledgeportal.worldbank.org/ — *Relevance:* Historical and projected climate data and country climate-risk profiles. — *Supports:* Country-level risk context for fund allocation weighting.

## 2. Sea-level science and attribution

**[IPCC6-CH9] Chapter 9: Ocean, Cryosphere and Sea Level Change** — Fox-Kemper, B., et al. (2021) — In: Climate Change 2021: The Physical Science Basis. Contribution of Working Group I to the Sixth Assessment Report of the IPCC (Cambridge University Press) — https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg1/chapter/chapter-9/ — *Relevance:* Definitive assessment of observed and projected sea-level rise, including Pacific islands. — *Supports:* Scientific basis for sea-level trend adjustment and L&D framing.

**[MERRIFIELD-2011] A Shift in Western Tropical Pacific Sea Level Trends during the 1990s** — Merrifield, M. A. (2011) — Journal of Climate, 24(15) — https://journals.ametsoc.org/view/journals/clim/24/15/2011jcli3932.1.xml — *Relevance:* Shows western tropical Pacific sea-level trends accelerated in the altimetry era, driven by trade-wind intensification. — *Supports:* Justification that Pacific sea level is regionally non-uniform, hence trend-adjusted (not fixed) trigger thresholds.

## 3. ENSO / El Nino effect on Pacific sea level

**[WIDLANSKY-2014] An Interhemispheric Tropical Sea Level Seesaw Due to El Nino** — Widlansky, M. J., Timmermann, A., McGregor, S., Stuecker, M. F., Cai, W. (2014) — Journal of Climate, 27(3) — https://journals.ametsoc.org/view/journals/clim/27/3/jcli-d-13-00276.1.xml — *Relevance:* Documents 20-30 cm sea-level drops around western Pacific islands during strong El Nino events. — *Supports:* ENSO adjustment term in trigger thresholds to avoid false (or missed) triggers from interannual variability.

**[WIDLANSKY-2015] Future Extreme Sea Level Seesaws in the Tropical Pacific** — Widlansky, M. J., Timmermann, A., Cai, W. (2015) — Science Advances, 1(8), e1500560 — https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.1500560 — *Relevance:* Extreme sea-level variability in the tropical Pacific is projected to change under warming, with direct island exposure implications. — *Supports:* Risk weighting and forward-looking trigger design for Pacific countries.

## 4. Saltwater intrusion and freshwater security in atolls

**[WERNER-2017] Hydrogeology and Management of Freshwater Lenses on Atoll Islands: Review of Current Knowledge and Research Needs** — Werner, A. D., Sharp, H. K., Galvis, S. C., Post, V. E. A., Sinclair, P. (2017) — Journal of Hydrology, 551 — https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022169417301269 (DOI: 10.1016/j.jhydrol.2017.02.047) — *Relevance:* Peer-reviewed review of atoll freshwater lens salinization mechanisms, covering Tuvalu, Kiribati and other atolls. — *Supports:* Impact narrative: sea-level rise damages freshwater security before inundation, justifying sea-level-based parametric triggers for L&D.

**[BAILEY-2014] Estimating Transient Freshwater Lens Dynamics for Atoll Islands of the Maldives** — Bailey, R. T., Khalil, A., Chatikavanij, V. (2014) — Journal of Hydrology, 515, 247–256 — https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0022169414003345 — *Relevance:* Models freshwater lens degradation under climatic variability and sea-level influence on atoll islands. — *Supports:* Quantitative link between sustained sea-level anomalies and water-security loss (trigger severity weighting).

## 5. Parametric risk financing precedents

**[PCRAFI] PCRAFI Combined Report: Advancing Disaster Risk Financing and Insurance in the Pacific** — World Bank / GFDRR / SPC — https://www.financialprotectionforum.org/publication/pcrafi-combined-report-advancing-disaster-risk-financing-and-insurance-in-the-pacific — *Relevance:* Foundational report on parametric risk modeling and trigger design for Pacific Island Countries. — *Supports:* Parametric trigger design principles and Pacific risk-pooling precedent.

**[PCRIC] PCRIC — Formation and History** — Pacific Catastrophe Risk Insurance Company — https://pcric.org/formation-and-history/ — *Relevance:* Official page on the operating parametric insurance facility for Pacific countries (successor to PCRAFI pilot). — *Supports:* Proof that sovereign parametric sea/cyclone risk pools work in the Pacific; model for HighTide's fund structure.

**[ARC] Africa RiskView Methodology** — African Risk Capacity (ARC Group) — https://www.arc.int/africa-riskview — *Relevance:* Official documentation of objective, satellite-based trigger thresholds and country-customized parameters. — *Supports:* Trigger design principles: objective, transparent, pre-agreed thresholds with customization per country.

## 6. Loss and Damage policy framework

**[UNFCCC-FRLD] Fund for Responding to Loss and Damage** — UNFCCC — https://unfccc.int/fund-for-responding-to-loss-and-damage — *Relevance:* Official page of the UNFCCC L&D Fund (operationalized at COP28). — *Supports:* L&D framing: HighTide as complementary, fast-disbursing funding arrangement.

**[COP28-1CP28] Decision 1/CP.28 and 5/CMA.5: Operationalization of the New Funding Arrangements, Including a Fund, for Responding to Loss and Damage** — UNFCCC (2023) — COP28, Dubai — https://unfccc.int/documents/634215 — *Relevance:* The decision text (FCCC/CP/2023/L.1–FCCC/PA/CMA/2023/L.1, adopted as Decisions 1/CP.28 and 5/CMA.5) establishing the Fund's governing instrument and arrangements. — *Supports:* Policy legitimacy for the L&D fund design and disbursement logic.

**[SNLD] Santiago Network** — UNFCCC — https://unfccc.int/santiago-network — *Relevance:* Official UNFCCC body catalyzing technical assistance for loss and damage (est. COP25). — *Supports:* L&D institutional framing and technical-assistance complement.

## 7. Forecasting methodology

**[FPP3] Forecasting: Principles and Practice (3rd edition)** — Hyndman, R. J., Athanasopoulos, G. (2021) — OTexts: Melbourne, Australia — https://otexts.com/fpp3/ — *Relevance:* Standard free textbook on time-series regression, trend estimation, and prediction intervals. — *Supports:* Forecast method (trend-adjusted sea-level forecasts) and prediction-interval-based trigger uncertainty.

**[STATSMOLS] OLSResults.get_prediction** — statsmodels developers — statsmodels.org API reference — https://www.statsmodels.org/devel/generated/statsmodels.regression.linear_model.OLSResults.get_prediction.html — *Relevance:* Official API for OLS point forecasts with confidence and observation (prediction) intervals. — *Supports:* Implementation of the statistical forecast and its uncertainty bands.

**[NIST-PI] NIST/SEMATECH e-Handbook of Statistical Methods, Section 4.1.3.2: Prediction** — NIST — https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/pmd/section1/pmd132.htm — *Relevance:* Authoritative distinction between prediction intervals (for a new observation) and confidence intervals (for a parameter). — *Supports:* Choice of prediction (not confidence) intervals for trigger thresholds.

## 8. Blockchain and parametric insurance / climate finance

**[EIP-712] EIP-712: Typed Structured Data Hashing and Signing** — Ethereum Improvement Proposals — https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-712 — *Relevance:* Official standard for signing structured, human-readable data (used for oracle attestations). — *Supports:* Verified sea-level reading signing and smart-contract trigger verification.

**[CHAINLINK] Chainlink Data Feeds Documentation** — Chainlink — https://docs.chain.link/data-feeds — *Relevance:* Official documentation of decentralized oracle networks delivering external data to smart contracts. — *Supports:* Oracle architecture for feeding verified sea-level data on-chain.

**[GOFFARD-2025] Collaborative and Parametric Insurance on the Ethereum Blockchain** — Goffard, P.-O., Loisel, S. (2025) — ASTIN Bulletin: Journal of the IAA, 56, 127–153 (published online 2025; issue 2026) — https://doi.org/10.1017/asb.2025.10078 (preprint: https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.05321) — *Relevance:* Peer-reviewed design of a blockchain-based parametric insurance scheme with smart-contract premiums and payouts. — *Supports:* Academic validation of the smart-contract parametric payout architecture.

**[CONDON-2017] Smart After All: Blockchain, Smart Contracts, Parametric Insurance, and Smart Energy Grids** — Cohn, A., West, T., Parker, C. (2017) — Georgetown Law Technology Review, 1(2), 273 — https://georgetownlawtechreview.org/smart-after-all-blockchain-smart-contracts-parametric-insurance-and-smart-energy-grids/GLTR-04-2017/ — *Relevance:* Legal-scholarly analysis of pairing parametric insurance with smart contracts for automatic execution. — *Supports:* Legal and design rationale for automatic, dispute-free parametric payouts.
